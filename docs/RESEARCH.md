# Research: keep notes in Supabase, pictures in Drive

**Best answer: keep the Phase 10 plan of encrypted notes on the Supabase free plan, and put encrypted pictures and backups in the owner's own Google Drive, behind two small swappable connectors.** Of the setups researched, this is the only one that holds several GB with no known need for a payment card and no way to be billed: when space runs out, things stop instead of charging. Google's free space is up to 15 GB, shared with Gmail and Photos. Supabase's docs do not state a card rule, so confirm that at sign-up. Cloudflare R2 has the best free storage (10 GB), but it needs a payment method and no hard spend stop was found, so it **does not meet a firm Rs 0 rule**. The same is true of Firebase Blaze. Nothing here was tested on a real iPad, and one test decides the plan: **does Google sign-in work inside the installed iPad app?** Run it before building sync. "End-to-end encrypted" (E2EE) means notes are locked on your device, so the storage company holds only unreadable data. It also means that if the passphrase, printed code and all devices are lost together, the notes are gone for good.

All web sources and npm versions were checked on **2026-11-01**. Most pages were read through a summarising tool, so re-check exact numbers on the live page before relying on them. No accounts were created. The deciding facts (Supabase limits and pausing, R2 limits, the Firebase change, Drive permission types, Google storage) and the order-key bug were checked a second time on 2026-11-01; no contradiction was found. **UNPROVEN** means no source or test backs the claim. "My reasoning" marks a judgement, not a sourced fact. Prices are in US dollars and were not converted to rupees.

## The setup has four parts, each with a different job

**Notes and recall cards** go into Supabase as encrypted rows, one per block as the roadmap says ([docs/ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md)). Supabase Free gives 500 MB of database, 1 GB of files and 5 GB of downloads a month ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). A database suits notes because it can refuse a stale save. That check is called compare-and-swap: "save only if nobody changed this since I last looked". Sync needs it, because the server cannot read notes and so cannot pick a winner. Each device decides (my reasoning).

**Pictures** go to Google Drive as encrypted files in a folder the app creates. Pictures rarely change, so Drive's weakness with overwrites barely matters. Use the `drive.file` permission, which covers only files the app made and needs only basic Google checks ([Choose scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/api-specific-auth)). Google's quotas are far above one person's use: 325,000 units a minute per user, with an edit costing 50 ([Usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)).

**Backups and the spare key** go to that same visible folder, not Drive's hidden app folder, which is deleted when the app is uninstalled from Drive ([App data guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/appdata)).

**The Windows PC keeps an independent copy.** A Google lockout would hit pictures, backups and the spare key together. So the PC should keep every picture locally and write a periodic encrypted backup to a folder you choose. Chrome on Windows can write back to a chosen file or folder, with a permission re-prompt ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/file-system-access)). This is my reasoning: it is the cheapest independent copy, and it proves your data can leave any vendor.

Drive alone could technically sync everything, but it should not be the live sync store. It has **no documented way to refuse a stale save** ([files.update](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/files/update)), and tools built on Drive report duplicates and conflict copies more than quota errors ([obsidian-gdrive-sync #18](https://github.com/stravo1/obsidian-gdrive-sync/issues/18)). Ready-made sync products do not fit either: none gives Rs 0, real E2EE and merging together. Dexie Cloud's free plan stops at 3 production users and 100 MB, with no E2EE mentioned in the pricing page or docs found ([pricing](https://dexie.org/cloud/pricing)). So write a thin sync layer yourself.

## Free plans cover about 5 GB of pictures if you shrink them first

Encrypted data cannot be compressed, so **shrink before encrypting**: for example, cap the long edge at 2000 px and convert to WebP or AVIF on the device. This is engineering judgement, and real savings need testing on your own pictures. Sync small thumbnails first and fetch full pictures on demand. As an illustration only, at an assumed 300 KB per shrunken picture, 1 GB holds about 3,500 pictures and 5 GB about 17,500.

Text has a tighter limit than it looks. Stored as base64 (a text-safe encoding), encrypted data grows 33%, so 500 MB holds about 375 MB of real content, before indexes and sync data. Store raw bytes where possible. Do not sync what can be rebuilt, such as the search index and link lists. History needs a size check, because `PageVersion` stores whole-page copies ([src/core/types.ts](../src/core/types.ts)), so syncing all of it could become the largest text cost (my reasoning, unmeasured).

**Backups use the same 15 GB.** At 500 MB of text, 7 daily plus 12 monthly full backups would take about 9.5 GB by my arithmetic. So back up text only, since pictures are already in Drive and on the PC, and keep fewer or incremental copies. The app should warn at 70-80% of each allowance, and can read Drive's quota through `about.get` ([about resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/about)).

## Moving later stays cheap if vendors only ever see locked boxes

Encrypted data moves as opaque bytes: nothing to convert, no re-encryption, and each copy can be checked by hash (my reasoning; **no public case study of an E2EE app changing vendors was found**). Public migrations mostly hurt on passwords, offline support, realtime rewrites and file addresses, including rich text whose old URLs left images broken for about a week ([Cotera](https://cotera.co/articles/supabase-vs-firebase-comparison.md); self-reported). A local-first encrypted app avoids most of these, but not the account move.

Supabase has the lowest lock-in here: standard Postgres dumps ([backup guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/migrating-within-supabase/backup-restore)), Docker self-hosting ([self-hosting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting)) and an Apache 2.0 main repo ([LICENSE](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/LICENSE)). Its exports need the CLI and Docker Desktop, which is heavy for a beginner, so the app's own encrypted export is the practical exit. Staying with a vendor and paying is the easy path, though no official page promises Supabase's address stays the same on upgrade (UNPROVEN). Drive has no central export, because data sits in each user's own account, so a move must run on a signed-in device ([App data guide](https://developers.google.com/drive/api/guides/appdata)). That is fine for one owner and real work at global scale.

Six habits keep you free: encrypt on the device; use your own `account_id` and store Google's `sub`, which is never reused or changed ([Google OpenID Connect](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect)); own the Google sign-in client in your own Google Cloud project; refer to pictures by app-made ids, never vendor URLs; import vendor code only inside the connectors; and build export early. Joplin shows one app can work across many storage services ([Joplin sync](https://joplinapp.org/help/apps/sync/)). To move: copy keys first, copy data and compare hashes, write to both for a while, switch, make every device re-check everything (a vendor's sync position means nothing at the next vendor), then keep the old copy read-only for a long time.

## Five catches matter more than the storage numbers

**Supabase pauses.** Seven quiet days pause the project, and sync stops until you click Resume in the Dashboard; restoring is possible for 1 year ([pausing docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). Older material says 90 days, so use the docs. Offline editing continues. A daily automatic request may prevent pausing, but that rests on a search summary (UNPROVEN). Free has **no automatic backups**, and database backups exclude Storage files ([backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)). At 95% of the fixed 2 GB disk the database goes read-only ([database size](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/database-size)), and sync-heavy write logs could reach that early (possible, untested). The pricing page says 500 MB per project while the docs say per organization, which does not matter with one project. Uptime promises are listed only for Enterprise ([pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)).

**Google sign-in is the largest unknown.** A browser-only app gets a token (a temporary pass) lasting about an hour, with no refresh token, and renews it through a Google dialog ([token model](https://developers.google.com/identity/oauth2/web/guides/use-token-model), [implicit flow](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/javascript-implicit-flow)). Do not put a client secret in the page to avoid this, because it breaks the no-secrets rule. Expect a tap about hourly; pictures may wait for that tap while notes keep syncing. Popup sign-in in installed iOS apps hung in old reports, which also conflict ([Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/blog/how-to-share-session-cookie-or-state-between-pwa-in-standalone-mode-and-safari-on-ios), [firebase-js-sdk #77](https://github.com/firebase/firebase-js-sdk/issues/77)). Links outside the app open in a Safari sheet, where sign-in state can go missing ([progressivewebapps.com](https://progressivewebapps.com/platforms/ios-safari/), [issue #1267](https://github.com/bulwarkmail/webmail/issues/1267)). **No Google or Apple document states what happens today on iPadOS 26 or 27 (UNPROVEN).** Set the Google project to "In production", because Testing mode expires consent every 7 days ([Manage app audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)).

Drive offers only polling from a browser, so changes appear within about 10 to 60 seconds with the app open (my estimate), never in the background ([Notifications](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/push)). Google's limits page says charges for exceeding quotas are planned for later in 2026 ([Usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)); whether that can touch a project with no billing account was not established. If your Drive stays over quota for 2 years or longer, content may be deleted after at least 3 months' notice ([help](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9312312)). Do not tidy the app's Drive folder by hand, and make the app cope if a file disappears.

**The iPad works only while the app is open.** Background Sync is unsupported on Safari through iOS 27.2 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/background-sync)), and Periodic Background Sync is not in Safari ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/web-apps/periodic-background-sync)). Sync on open, on return to the app, on reconnect and after edits, and show "last synced". Safari and the installed app **do not share data** ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/)), so notes typed in a Safari tab will not appear in the installed app. The installed app is exempt from the 7-day deletion rule ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/)), but storage pressure can still remove its data unless persistence is granted, which Safari decides silently ([storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Space is ample, at up to 60% of disk per site ([storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). The iPad cannot write to a chosen file ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/native-filesystem-api)), so each iPad backup file needs a tap. IndexedDB (the browser database under Dexie) has crashed on iOS, with data loss reported in a bug closed as fixed yet still reported in October 2025 ([Bug 273827](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=273827)). Safari 27.0 lists recovery fixes ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-for-safari-27-0/)). Existing installs may never pick up manifest changes such as name, icon or start address ([progressivewebapps.com](https://progressivewebapps.com/platforms/ios-safari/)).

**Encryption is a home-made design until reviewed.** Published breaks of encrypted services come from a dishonest server exploiting the design ([ePrint 2026/058](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/058), abstract only; [ePrint 2024/546](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/546), snippet). Copy the proven split: a random master key locks data, and each unlock method separately locks that key, so a passphrase change touches no notes ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cryptographic_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html), [Standard Notes](https://standardnotes.com/help/security/encryption)). The roadmap's two recovery routes are sound in structure, with one weak point: **anyone holding the Drive copy can guess passphrases offline, without limit, so it is only as strong as the passphrase.** Use a generated four-to-six word passphrase with a strength check (UNPROVEN figures), and a printed code of at least 128 random bits. Keep a locked key copy in Supabase for new devices, with Drive as the spare, and device pairing as a third route.

Recommended settings are AES-256-GCM with a 128-bit tag and a fresh random 96-bit IV (a one-use number) on every save ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AesGcmParams)), and Argon2id (a passphrase-hardening method) through WebAssembly, starting at 64 MiB, t=3, p=1, tuned on the iPad ([OWASP floors](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html)). Those starting numbers and the per-object key construction are UNPROVEN. PBKDF2 at 600,000 rounds is only a fallback. Bind each record's id, type and version into the encryption so the server cannot swap blocks ([Standard Notes](https://standardnotes.com/help/security/encryption)), and authenticate picture chunks in order ([Proton](https://proton.me/blog/protondrive-security)). Keep search on the device.

Three limits remain. A non-extractable key stops theft of key bytes, not misuse: injected script can still decrypt while it runs ([W3C Web Crypto](https://w3c.github.io/webcrypto/), [blog, weak source](https://zerokey.vercel.app/blog/non-extractable-keys-xss)). Whoever controls the hosted code could ship code that reads keys, so never promise "no one could ever read this" (UNPROVEN as a citation). And encryption hides content, not activity: the server still sees times, sizes, counts and your account ([Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/help/sync/security)). For health-related notes, encrypt titles, tags and picture names too. No legal assessment was done. An outside audit costs money and needs owner approval.

**Merging is simple now and smarter later.** For Phase 10 keep newest-edit-wins per block, with the loser copied to History. Use a hybrid logical clock (HLC: device time plus a counter) to pick the winner, and a base revision (the version you started from) to detect a true conflict. Plain device time lets a fast clock always win ([TopGun](https://topgun-f45.pages.dev/blog/hybrid-logical-clocks)). Keep a block's content and its place on separate clocks, so a move and an edit both survive. Database cells merge per cell, recall review logs merge by adding rows, and card schedules rebuild by replaying the logs, which improves on Anki's "most recent answer" result ([Anki forum](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/the-way-reviews-are-merge-is-strange-dont-you-think/27109)); replaying across old `fixed` and `fsrs` logs is UNPROVEN. **Delete forever always wins.** Expected flaws: a one-word fix can lose out as a whole block, and splitting a block on one device while editing it on the other can duplicate text. Show a "this page was merged, see History" notice, since silent merges are a real complaint ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/obsidian-sync-add-a-notification-warning-when-sync-merge-with-conflicts-happens/14943)).

In Phase 15, move pages one at a time to Yjs, the only CRDT (a data type that merges itself) BlockNote supports ([BlockNote docs](https://www.blocknotejs.org/docs/features/collaboration)). Proton Docs shows Yjs with E2EE can ship ([Yjs](https://github.com/yjs/yjs/)), but the server cannot compact encrypted logs, so devices must ([Secsync](https://github.com/serenity-kit/secsync)). `blocksToYDoc` imports existing pages, but only one device may do it, or the page duplicates ([BlockNote](https://www.blocknotejs.org/docs/reference/editor/yjs-utilities), [Büsing](https://morizbuesing.com/blog/initializing-a-yjs-document-with-a-common-value)). Yjs 14 is still a release candidate, so do not add Yjs in Phase 10.

## Sixteen open questions need a real device or account

1. Does Google sign-in (Supabase Auth, and the Google popup for Drive) complete and return to the installed app on current iPadOS? Popup or redirect?
2. Can the Drive token renew without a visible window or tap (`prompt: 'none'` or empty), including under Safari tracking prevention?
3. Can one Google sign-in serve both Supabase and Drive, or are two needed? (Not researched.)
4. Does an "In production" app with these permissions show any warning, and does the Google project setup ask for a card? Does Supabase sign-up?
5. How often is `persist()` granted on iPad, and does the installed app survive storage pressure?
6. Does removing the Home Screen icon delete app data on iPadOS 26/27? Assume yes.
7. Do `<a download>` and Web Share with files work in the installed app, and how large can an export be?
8. Are large pictures in IndexedDB safe on your iPad? Which Safari has the blob fix ([Bug 292142](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=292142))? Does Bug 273827 still occur on 26/27?
9. How long does Argon2id take, and how much memory does it use, on your iPad?
10. Are Web Crypto Argon2 and X25519 available? Assume not; use WebAssembly and ECDH P-256.
11. How long does a Drive write take to appear in `changes.list`, and does `drive.file` limit that list to the app's files?
12. How quickly can a picture be fetched from Drive and decrypted (CORS, range requests)?
13. Does a daily request stop Supabase pausing, and does revoking Google access delete hidden-folder data?
14. How much do your real pictures shrink, how big does your text get, and how much of the 15 GB do Gmail and Photos already use?
15. Do Scribble, every Magic Keyboard shortcut and two windows work with BlockNote in the installed app?
16. For Phase 15: do BlockNote block ids survive `blocksToYDoc` and merges? Recall cards depend on them.

## Conclusion

The real limit is not free gigabytes. It is whether a browser-only app can stay signed in to two services on an iPad, and whether you keep your recovery words safe. The iPad test is the cheapest step in this report and the one most able to change it. Free plans also tightened in 2026: Firebase dropped file storage on Spark ([FAQ](https://firebase.google.com/docs/storage/faqs-storage-changes-announced-sept-2024)) and Appwrite added pausing ([changelog](https://appwrite.io/changelog/entry/2026-02-20-1.md)). Treat today's numbers as temporary.

That is why the two connectors matter as much as today's vendors. With data locked before it leaves the device, a later move is a copy job, and the first paid step stays small and optional: picture storage on R2, if you ever lift the Rs 0 rule.
