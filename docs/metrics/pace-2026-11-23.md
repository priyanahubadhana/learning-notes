## What the numbers say

1. **We are building the method, not the product.** Two phases of tooling (1A, 1M) sit in front of the first feature phase. Six in ten commits this week were documents.
2. **Specs are heavy.** One spec rewrite (U0b) cost 3.5 h and three review rounds before any code. A builder window of 100K spends a large share reading.
3. **The review loop doubles the cost.** The reviewer window is often bigger than the builder window, and most FAILs came from the process (merge order, instructions), not from the work.
4. **Tests run serially per task.** 20 of 20 on two browsers inside every builder round, one WebKit run at a time on this PC: U0a's final run alone takes over an hour.
5. **One lane in practice.** U0c waits for U0a, 1M waits for U0a, 1B waits for 1M.

## Picks to speed up (each with the case against; the owner decides)

- **P1 Shrink 1M to its two load-bearing parts** (agent files, the stop hook) and start the 1B notebook lane beside it. Against: 1M rows 1 to 11 were confirmed by the owner; this is a plan change (replan counter +1).
- **P2 Two spec sizes.** Template v2 in full only for screens the owner sees; a one-page card (goal, rules, three scenarios, done when) for internal parts. Against: METHOD-v2 says every feature on template v2.
- **P3 One review, fixes checked by diff.** A second reviewer window only when a fix touches a screen. Against: fewer eyes on code.
- **P4 Repeats at merge, not per round.** 3 of 3 in the builder's rounds; 20 of 20 once, on CI, at the merge. Against: a flaky test is found later, at the merge.
- **P5 Rule weight.** Count the tokens a builder reads before its first edit; cut briefs and rule files until that is under 20K. Against: a builder may miss a rule.
